Checklists that behave

Tables and images

Grow a table by rows and columns. Add a photo, pick from the gallery, or paste a screenshot — each one encrypted on its own key.

What we protect, and what we don't.

A privacy app should tell you its limits as clearly as its strengths. Here are both, taken from the security document in the repository.

vaultp / SECURITY.mdMIT

// protected

  • ✓Database encrypted with SQLCipherAES-256, on the phone and the desktop
  • ✓Every file on a key of its ownAES-256-GCM, bound to that file's id
  • ✓Sync carries ciphertext onlyeach segment bound to its device and sequence
  • ✓Passphrase never stored or sentPBKDF2-SHA512, 210,000 iterations
  • ✓No account, no server, no analyticsthere is nothing to breach but your own device

// known and accepted

  • !Lose the passphrase, lose the vaultno reset and no copy anywhere — that is the point
  • !An exported backup is unencryptedthe app says so at the button
  • !The device is trustedmalware on an unlocked device can read it
  • !Sync storage sees names and sizesnot contents
  • !Desktop builds are not code-signedverify what you download
passphrase  ─ PBKDF2-SHA512 ×210,000 ─▶  wrapping key
                                           │ unwraps
data key (random, never derived)  ◀───────┘
    │ HKDF
    ├─▶ database     SQLCipher, AES-256
    ├─▶ files        AES-256-GCM, one key per file
    ├─▶ sync         AES-256-GCM, per segment
    └─▶ credentials  your Drive sign-in, if you use it

! Not independently audited. Written carefully and tested across both platforms — weigh that against what you store.

Sync

Your phone and your computer, in step — through storage you choose.

There is no VaultP server to sync through. Point both devices at somewhere you already trust; it only ever receives sealed bytes.

A folder

Syncthing, a shared drive, a USB stick — anything that keeps a folder the same in two places.

Your own Google Drive

Through your own Google project, scoped to the one folder the app creates. Google stores ciphertext.

Or not at all

One device, fully offline, forever. Nothing about the app assumes a network.
